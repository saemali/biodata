How to Write a Biodata for Marriage, Step by Step

Most people find writing about themselves awkward, and parents worry about sounding either too plain or too proud. This step-by-step method keeps it honest, warm and quick.

Step 1: Gather the facts first

Before writing a word, collect: - Full name, date of birth, height, city - Education (degree, college, year) and work (role, sector) - Parents’ names and occupations, siblings - Horoscope details, if your family uses them, copied exactly from the kundli - Diet, faith practice, family type (joint/nuclear)

Accuracy matters more than style. A wrong birth time or a typo in a name is the most common, and most embarrassing, mistake.

Step 2: Decide who is “speaking”

  • Parent-written: use the 3rd person (“She is a…”, “He enjoys…”).
  • Candidate-written: use the 1st person (“I’m a…”). Pick one and stay with it throughout.

Step 3: Write the “About” paragraph

Use a simple pattern: work + interests + values + hope.

Traditional: “Aditya is a design engineer who enjoys trekking in the Sahyadris and playing the tabla. He is calm, dependable and close to his family.”

Modern: “I’m a UX designer who loves sketching and long drives. I value honesty, kindness and laughter, and I’d love to build a warm home with someone who respects both careers.”

(Both are fictional examples.)

A useful trick is to ask three family members or friends for three words that describe the person, then use the ones that come up most.

Step 4: Phrase expectations as hopes

Instead of Try
“Must be highly educated” “We hope to connect with a well-educated family”
“Only from our community” “A partner who shares our values” (or “Community is flexible”)
“Should be fair and slim” (leave it out entirely)

Step 5: Words to avoid

  • Superlatives: “extremely”, “very handsome”, “most beautiful”
  • Wealth signals: “rich family”, “big house”
  • Complexion and appearance ratings
  • Anything you cannot back up

Step 6: Check privacy

Decide how much detail to share before the biodata starts travelling.

Privacy tips before you forward it

  • Show the city, not your home address.
  • Give one parent’s number rather than the candidate’s.
  • Show income as a range, or “well settled”, if you prefer.
  • Keep a photo-free copy for wider forwarding.

Step 7: Proofread and format

Read it aloud. Check every number. Use a clean A4 layout with readable font sizes, and export a PDF small enough to send easily.
