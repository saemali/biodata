Muslim Biodata for Marriage: Format and Etiquette

A rishta biodata for a Muslim family differs from many other formats in a few important ways. Most notably, it has no horoscope section, and it gives more space to faith and family values.

Typical structure

  1. Opening: many families begin with Bismillah-ir-Rahman-ir-Rahim
  2. Personal details: name, date of birth, height, education, profession, city, native place
  3. Religious details: - Sect/maslak (for example Sunni Hanafi or Shia), if the family wishes to mention it - Religious practice: namaz, roza - Biradari (optional; many families now leave it out or write “flexible”)
  4. Family details: parents with occupations, siblings and their marital status
  5. About the candidate
  6. Partner expectations
  7. Contact person

Writing the “About” paragraph

Keep it sincere and simple, and mention deen only in the way the family actually practises it. A fictional example:

“Ayaan is a practising Muslim working as a civil engineer in Hyderabad. He values deen, family and education, and hopes to find a partner who shares these values.”

Expectations, phrased gently

“We hope to find an educated, practising partner from a respectful family, who values deen and family.”

Etiquette

  • Be accurate about practice. Families appreciate honesty more than idealised descriptions.
  • Avoid complexion and appearance language.
  • Keep wealth out. Education, work and character matter more.
  • Use respectful forms for parents, as your family normally does.

Privacy tips before you forward it

  • Show the city, not your home address.
  • Give one parent’s number rather than the candidate’s.
  • Show income as a range, or “well settled”, if you prefer.
  • Keep a photo-free copy for wider forwarding.

Frequently asked questions

Should a Muslim biodata include a horoscope? Usually not. Muslim rishta biodatas generally skip horoscope fields and focus on religious practice and family instead.

Is mentioning sect or biradari necessary? No. Both are optional. Include them only if your family considers them important.

How should religious practice be described? Simply and accurately, for example ‘regular namaz, keeps roza’. Avoid exaggeration.

Can the candidate write the biodata in the 1st person? Yes. Both 1st person and parent-written 3rd person are common.

Final checklist before sharing

  • Names spelled as in official documents
  • Date, time and place of birth double-checked
  • Horoscope or community fields copied exactly (if used)
  • Phone number tested
  • No complexion words or superlatives
  • PDF under 2 MB, A4, readable when printed in black and white
  • A photo-free copy saved for wider sharing
