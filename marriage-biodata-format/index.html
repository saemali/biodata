Marriage Biodata Format: What to Include and in What Order

A marriage biodata is often the first thing another family sees. A clear, honest one shows care, while a cluttered or boastful one can put people off before anyone meets. This guide gives you a format that works for most families, plus etiquette that keeps it dignified.

The standard order

Most biodatas follow this order, and families find it easy to read: 1. Header: an optional invocation line or a simple title (“Marriage Biodata”) 2. Photo and name 3. Personal details: date of birth, height, education, profession, city, native place, diet 4. Horoscope details (optional): time and place of birth, gotra, rashi, nakshatra, manglik status, or your community’s equivalent 5. Education and career 6. Family details: parents’ names and occupations, siblings and their marital status 7. About the candidate, in a short paragraph 8. Partner expectations 9. Contact person

Keep it to one A4 page, or two if you add a horoscope or family-tree page.

What each section should say

Personal details: facts only. Write “B.Com, 2019” rather than “highly educated”. Height in feet and inches is common.

Education and career: degree, institution (optional) and year. For work, the role and sector are enough. The employer name and income are optional, and many families show income as a range or write “well settled”.

Family: name each parent with their occupation (or “retired” / “homemaker”). List siblings with education and whether they are married. Native place and grandparents are a nice touch for families who value roots.

About: three to five sentences on who the person is: work, interests, values. Let the facts speak.

Partner expectations: phrase them as hopes (“We hope to connect with…”), not demands (“Must be…”).

Etiquette that makes a good impression

  • Stay humble. Avoid superlatives like “extremely beautiful”, “very handsome” or “rich family”.
  • Leave complexion out. It adds nothing about who a person is.
  • Never stretch facts. Families verify things, and an exaggeration costs trust.
  • Copy horoscope details exactly from the kundli or jathagam, and check them with your pandit or astrologer.
  • Proofread twice, especially names, dates and phone numbers.

A short fictional example

About Riya: Riya works as a product analyst in Gurugram and enjoys Kathak, reading and weekend cooking with her mother. She is thoughtful, keeps her word, and values a home where both partners support each other’s work and family.

Partner expectations: We hope to connect with a well-educated, kind and family-oriented young man with similar values. Community is flexible.

Format variations

The order above suits most families. Some communities add their own fields: Muslim biodatas usually skip horoscope details and list sect and religious practice, Sikh biodatas may include Amritdhari/Kesdhari status, and Christian biodatas often list denomination and parish. Privacy tips before you forward it

  • Show the city, not your home address.
  • Give one parent’s number rather than the candidate’s.
  • Show income as a range, or “well settled”, if you prefer.
  • Keep a photo-free copy for wider forwarding.
