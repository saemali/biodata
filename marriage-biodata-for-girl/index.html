Marriage Biodata for a Girl: Dignified, Humble and Safe

Families preparing a daughter’s biodata often worry about two things: sounding “too forward”, and how widely her photo and details will travel. Here is how to get both right.

What to include

  • Name, date of birth, height, city, native place
  • Education and career, stated plainly. Her qualifications and work deserve the same clear space as anyone’s.
  • Diet, faith practice, family type
  • Family: parents’ names and occupations, siblings
  • Horoscope details, if your family uses them, copied exactly
  • A short “About” paragraph and gentle partner expectations

What to leave out

  • Complexion and appearance ratings. They say nothing about who she is, and many families now find them uncomfortable.
  • Superlatives like “very beautiful” or “perfect homemaker”.
  • Exact salary, unless you want it shown. A range or “well settled” is fine.
  • Home address. The city is enough.

Writing her “About” paragraph

Focus on what she does, what she enjoys and what she values. A balanced, fictional example:

“Kavya is a staff nurse in Chennai who chose the profession because she likes caring for people. She learns Carnatic vocal music on weekends, enjoys temple visits with her family, and is known among friends for her patience and good humour.”

If she is writing it herself in the 1st person, that is perfectly acceptable and increasingly common.

Career plans and expectations

If she plans to keep working after marriage, it helps to say so gently and early:

“She plans to continue her career and hopes for a partner who respects both careers and traditions.”

Partner expectations work best as hopes: “We hope to connect with a sincere, well-educated family that values kindness and mutual respect.”

Privacy matters more here

  • Share a photo-free version with groups and wider circles.
  • Give a parent’s number as the contact.
  • Keep the full version with photo for families you are seriously talking to.

Common mistakes

  • Letting relatives add flattering adjectives at the last minute
  • Leaving out her education or work to seem “modest”
  • Sharing an editable file that anyone can change
  • Typos in the horoscope details

Frequently asked questions

Should a girl’s biodata mention complexion? We recommend leaving it out. It says nothing about character, education or values.

Can she write it herself? Yes. A 1st-person biodata is increasingly common and often sounds more natural.

Final checklist before sharing

  • Names spelled as in official documents
  • Date, time and place of birth double-checked
  • Horoscope or community fields copied exactly (if used)
  • Phone number tested
  • No complexion words or superlatives
  • PDF under 2 MB, A4, readable when printed in black and white
  • A photo-free copy saved for wider sharing
