Second Marriage Biodata: Writing It with Dignity

A biodata for a second marriage (after divorce or the loss of a spouse) needs the same clarity as any other, with extra care in a few places. Honest, calm wording builds trust far better than avoiding the topic.

What to include

  • All the usual details: name, date of birth, height, education, work, city, family
  • Marital status, stated plainly: “Divorced” or “Widowed”. There is no need to explain why in the biodata itself.
  • Children (optional): for example “One son, age 6, living with me”. Share as much as you are comfortable with.
  • About the candidate and partner expectations

Gentle, honest wording

Fictional examples:

“Neha is a chartered accountant in Indore and a devoted mother to her seven-year-old daughter. She enjoys gardening and classical music, and hopes to build a warm, stable home with a kind and understanding partner.”

“After the loss of his wife in 2022, Sunil has focused on his work as a pharmacist and on his family. He is calm, caring and ready to begin a new chapter with someone who values companionship and respect.”

Partner expectations

“We hope to connect with a mature, understanding person who is open to a family with a child.” “Marital status of the partner is not a concern; values and kindness matter most.”

Etiquette

  • Don’t hide marital status. It will come up, and hiding it erodes trust.
  • Avoid blame or detail about a previous marriage. That conversation belongs in person, later.
  • Protect children’s privacy. Leave out children’s names and photos.
  • Keep the same humble tone: no superlatives and no complexion language.

Privacy tips before you forward it

  • Show the city, not your home address.
  • Give one parent’s number rather than the candidate’s.
  • Show income as a range, or “well settled”, if you prefer.
  • Keep a photo-free copy for wider forwarding.

Frequently asked questions

Should I mention the reason for divorce? No. Stating ‘Divorced’ is enough. The details are better shared in person, once both families are talking.

Should children be mentioned? It is usually better to mention them, with their age and who they live with, but leave out names and photos to protect their privacy.

Can the format be the same as a first-marriage biodata? Yes. The structure is the same; only the marital status and family sections change.

How do I phrase expectations? Gently, for example ‘We hope to connect with an understanding person who is open to a family with a child.’

Final checklist before sharing

  • Names spelled as in official documents
  • Date, time and place of birth double-checked
  • Horoscope or community fields copied exactly (if used)
  • Phone number tested
  • No complexion words or superlatives
  • PDF under 2 MB, A4, readable when printed in black and white
  • A photo-free copy saved for wider sharing
