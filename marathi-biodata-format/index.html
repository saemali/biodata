Marathi Biodata Format for Marriage

Marathi families often prepare a marriage biodata for elders and relatives. Here is what a Marathi biodata typically contains. (Our own templates use English field labels, with the invocation line in Devanagari.)

Typical structure

  1. Invocation (optional): “॥ श्री गणेशाय नमः ॥” (some families use ॥ श्री स्वामी समर्थ ॥ or ॐ नमः शिवाय)
  2. Personal details: name, date of birth, time and place of birth, height, education, occupation, city
  3. Native village (gaav): commonly included
  4. Horoscope: gotra, kul, kuldaivat (family deity), raas (moon sign), nakshatra with charan (1–4), gan (dev / manushya / rakshas), nadi (aadya / madhya / antya) and mangal. Devak is listed mainly by Maratha, Kunbi and Mali families, not by every family. Brahmin families usually list gotra and pravar instead
  5. Family: parents with occupations, siblings, and often the maternal family (mama’s side)
  6. About the candidate
  7. Partner expectations
  8. Contact

Marathi terms are given for reference; check with your family’s usage.

Writing it well

  • Honorifics: Marathi biodatas often use Shri for men, Sau. for married women and Ku. for unmarried women. Follow your family’s usage.
  • Humble tone: let education and work speak for themselves. Avoid superlatives and complexion words.
  • Horoscope accuracy: copy every field exactly from the patrika and confirm it with your astrologer.
  • Native place: a line about your village or district helps families connect.

Frequently asked questions

Is devak or nadi compulsory? No. Devak is mainly listed by Maratha, Kunbi and Mali families, and many families skip nadi too. Include these fields only if your family uses them, and copy them exactly from the patrika.

Can I get the biodata in Marathi? Our templates and done-for-you service use English labels only. Many families use the English version and keep Marathi for the invocation line. A Marathi copy would need to be made separately.

Should the native village be mentioned? Many Marathi families like to see it, because it helps them place the family. A district or taluka name is enough.

Do I need to mention caste? No. Community is optional, and many families now leave it out.

Final checklist before sharing

  • Names spelled as in official documents
  • Date, time and place of birth double-checked
  • Horoscope or community fields copied exactly (if used)
  • Phone number tested
  • No complexion words or superlatives
  • PDF under 2 MB, A4, readable when printed in black and white
  • A photo-free copy saved for wider sharing
